Applied research · documented under SR&ED · 2022 to 2024

The NLU program, as it was written down.

SR&ED is Canada's federal program for scientific research and experimental development. A company files after its fiscal year closes and answers the Canada Revenue Agency's three questions: what technological uncertainty it faced, what systematic work it did to resolve it, and what it learned. There is no proposal stage, so a filing is only as good as the record behind it. Side documented its conversation-AI work under SR&ED for fiscal 2022 through 2024. This page is the engineering in that record: the uncertainties as written at the time, the data-capture process that produced the corpus, the model configurations that ran, and what happened.

01

The uncertainties we wrote down

Five appear across the 2022 scientific report and the fiscal 2023 technical report. The quotations are the language of the time.

  • No source of truth for the presale market. "No such data sources exist for the preconstruction market." The data was disjoint, uncurated, inconsistent across sources and changed weekly, so standard scraping and aggregation could not produce one record per project. The team had to invent both the normalization rules and the way to validate them.
  • No architecture to copy for autonomous workflow navigation. "There are no standardized system architectures that can be applied in this domain to automatically navigate prospective clients through personalized workflows all the way to finalizing transactions." The 2022 answer was a CRM workflow builder, and the team had already run one until it stopped scaling.
  • Latency and combinatorics pulled against each other. The platform had to "handle 1000s of unique interactions autonomously and respond to engagements in less than 1 second" against a combinatorial input space. Desk analysis cannot settle that. It has to be built far enough to be measured.
  • No prior intent schema for this domain. The objective was "a minimal yet complete intent / slot taxonomy" that could map at least 95 percent of inbound queries, and the recorded uncertainty was that no schema existed for British Columbia presale real estate. A taxonomy that missed a class of buyer question would waste every hour of labelling after it.
  • Unknown sample size and annotation budget. "Unknown sample size required for convergence", "Unclear annotation budget", and "Lack of explainability hampers iteration" are recorded as uncertainties about the research process itself. Annotation was done by hand by one or two people, so sample efficiency per intent set the cost and the calendar.
02

The data-capture workflow

A written SOP, "Real-Time Conversational Data Capture and AI-Training Workflow", took effect on October 1, 2022. It defines a human-in-the-loop capture loop with owners, service levels, a sampling rate and a retention policy. Individuals are named in the original; they appear here as roles.

  1. Scope and consent. Inbound and outbound calls, SMS and web chat with prospective buyers, British Columbia presale projects only. Excluded by rule: internal meetings, non-real-estate enquiries, and "interactions without explicit recording consent". A consent script sat beside the taxonomy and the transcript schema.
  2. Mandatory capture. Five items had to come out of every conversation, each mapped to a named entity: target area (city), bedrooms (bdrm), budget (budget or around_budget), desired completion, and callback details. The SOP designates five of these as critical entities.
  3. Dynamic prompting. "If model confidence < 0.75 for any critical entity, ask a clarifier." A phrase like "around six hundred" was tagged around_budget and confirmed as a numeric range on the call, which is why the domain carries three budget slots.
  4. Objections and escalation, written as a map. "Just looking" routes to warm_give_info; "prices too high" to user_budget_too_high; "call me later" to give_call_meeting_info. Legal and compliance questions were warm-transferred and tagged handoff_broker. Abusive language was tagged mood_unhappy and capture stopped.
  5. Live tagging. Hot-keys wrote timestamped markers into a sidecar file during the call: city, bedrooms, budget.
  6. Post-call pipeline with a service level on every step. Speech to text within 15 minutes; transcript clean-up at 30 minutes per hour of audio; same-day labelling in Rasa X; QA review within 24 hours against a diff; a nightly ETL job.
  7. QA sampling. The annotation lead spot-checked 10 percent of the specialist's labels, computed inter-rater agreement on the overlap, and returned corrections. Validators and slot-tag consistency scripts ran over each batch as a separate check.
  8. Nightly retraining. A nightly job built new_examples.parquet and fine-tuned the DIET classifier for 200 epochs. Training metrics posted to a channel automatically; misclassifications went back to the specialist for next-day correction. A bad prediction became a corrected label inside 24 hours.
  9. Retention and encryption. "Recordings stored encrypted (AES-256), deleted after 180 days; transcripts kept 3 years." One read-only access role. Consent logs kept with transcript metadata so any transcript traces back to the consent that permitted it.
  10. Ownership. The CTO owned the taxonomy, the annotation guidelines and the retraining trigger. A conversational data specialist ran the SOP in real time and delivered daily labelled batches. Dev-ops owned storage, the nightly ETL and pipeline health. Changes were logged against a tracked component.

The SOP set two operating targets for the capture work: critical entities captured on at least 95 percent of calls, and objections resolved into a scheduled call at least 70 percent of the time. Both are targets written into the process before the work ran.

03

Three pipelines, one task

The production Rasa system carried three layers, each with its own configuration, all dated February 2023, all aimed at the same problem: classify a presale buyer's intent and pull the slot values out of the message. The task, the taxonomy and the training data were held fixed. The model family and the featurization varied.

Two axes were under test. One was the model family: a neural joint intent-and-entity model, a linear classifier over character n-grams, and a pretrained transformer featurizer feeding a larger DIET run with entities pushed onto a more regularized CRF. The other was the cost of being wrong: the two layers that talk to buyers carry a 0.9 fallback and a rule policy, so a low-confidence turn becomes a clarifying question; the end-to-end layer drops both and lets the learned policy run.

04

The domain, in the model's own vocabulary

The production domain file carries 45 intents and 34 entity types, grown from the 18 and 12 the 2022 taxonomy started with. The names show where the modelling went. Twelve of them:

  • warm_give_info: the buyer volunteers preferences in free text, usually bedrooms, city and budget in one message.
  • lead_ask_completion_date: when the building completes, which in presale can be years after the contract.
  • lead_ask_payment_structure: the deposit schedule, paid in instalments over construction.
  • lead_ask_assignment_fee: what the developer charges to assign the contract to someone else.
  • lead_ask_assign_before_completion: whether assignment is permitted at all before completion.
  • lead_ask_flip_before_completion: whether the unit can be resold before completion, the speculative case, a different question from assignment.
  • lead_ask_price_Sq: price per square foot, the comparison unit between projects.
  • lead_ask_maintenance_fee: the monthly strata charge.
  • lead_ask_concrete_building: concrete or wood frame, which drives cost, insurance and resale.
  • lead_ask_ev_parking: whether the stall supports electric-vehicle charging.
  • lead_ask_airbnb_allowed_yn: whether short-stay rental is permitted under the bylaws.
  • multi_questions: several of the above in one message, given its own class so a stacked question is something the model can hit.

Two naming patterns carry design decisions. The _yn suffix marks the same question asked back as a yes-or-no confirmation after the assistant has answered once, a different dialogue state that needed its own class. And budget, around_budget and max_budget are three slots, one per way buyers state affordability, one of which the SOP required the operator to resolve on the call.

05

What the record says happened

Generation one, rule-based. A hierarchical conditional workflow in the CRM's workflow builder, beginning "by mapping over 50 branching nodes representing common queries and parameters, which later grew to 120+ nodes". Over six weeks and 2,000-plus unique engagements it held a 1 percent conversion rate, the industry baseline. What ended it was maintenance: "Translating the rapidly expanding conditional framework into code became unmanageable."

Generation two, in-house NLU. Each level of the decision tree was treated as a conversational turn and learned. Training data came from 8,000 SMS and web-chat turns captured between May 2021 and August 2022, de-identified and segmented before annotation; the SOP above formalized the loop from October 2022. The Rasa system ran the three pipelines against a domain that had grown to 45 intents. Side reported conversion rising to "over 3% while maintaining 80% workflow autonomy", with the system handling more than 1,000 conversations a day. The same record names the limits the models still had: divergent or irrelevant replies, and unreliable classification of the granular specifications a buyer gives when close to a decision.

A separate 2024 workstream on transformer lead scoring did not ship. A UBC Master of Data Science capstone team trained three models to score a conversation's warmth: a zero-shot DeBERTa-v3 classifier, a DistilBERT transfer-learning model, and a fine-tuned DistilBERT. None of the three reached a usable recall on the hot class. The team wrote the reasons plainly: too few hot conversations to learn from, a warm label whose definition had drifted over the life of the CRM, and phone conversations they never had. Their conclusion was that the end goals were not reached with the data available. The generation after it was rebuilt on large language models.

06

My role

I was the program director. I specified the problem and the targets, funded the work, set up and directed the capstone collaboration, ran the commercial side that produced the conversations the models learned from, and made the calls about what to build next when a generation ran out.

The Rasa system, the domain model, the annotation taxonomy and the retraining pipeline were built by the internal team, led by the CTO. The warmth classifiers were built by the capstone team. Side's own documents from the period credit the conversational-AI development to the CTO and the supervised warmth model to the capstone collaboration.
