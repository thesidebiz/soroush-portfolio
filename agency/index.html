Seven client systems on one platform, as built.

Side Studios is the studio subsidiary of Side, the company I co-founded in Vancouver in 2021. This page covers the studio's client work: the CRM, the calendars, the messaging, and increasingly the voice and chat agents, all on a white-label GoHighLevel platform. Counts here are from the estate as it stood in September 2026.

The book splits into three eras. Hand-built work from 2021 to 2023, with the first LLM deployments. A productized snapshot era in 2024 and 2025, where one template was installed across the client base. Then 2026, where the work shifted from many workflows to a few well-guarded agents that call workflows. The seven systems below show how the estate is built.

01

The AI receptionist, three generations

A martial-arts academy with a legacy booking platform. Calls used to go to voicemail.

Voice agent · tool use · productized

From a scripted receptionist to a white-label template

The live agent has twelve configured actions, ten of which are structured in-call extraction into a typed field schema created for it in one batch: caller, child's age, program of interest, preferred days, consent to text. SMS follow-up is consent-gated inside the call. A post-call workflow trigger hands the structured record to the booking flow. Two earlier generations are kept in the account as the record of what changed.

  • Ten calendars created programmatically from the client's practice-management system, with a matching field cluster that keeps the raw guardian record as JSON so nothing is lost against a schema we do not control.
  • A 95-question knowledge base that refreshes from the client's own site, and hand-authored structured data for the business.
  • The same agent definition abstracted behind named template variables (business, owner, channels) and re-instantiated for a second brand. One definition, one variable contract, N clients.

What it shows: tool-use design (extract, do not chat), consent as a first-class step, and the move from a project to a product.

02

The guarded agent

An industrial laser manufacturer whose inbound calls are technical and whose claims are regulated.

Voice agent · retrieval architecture · guardrails

Five knowledge bases, one of them a claim policy

Retrieval is partitioned by function rather than dumped into one corpus: products, applications, service, company, and a dedicated guardrails and claim-policy corpus. The prompt carries an enumerated list of things the agent must not claim, a named uncertainty protocol ("I do not want to guess on that"), an identity rail so the agent never presents as a human engineer, and a bounded call-transfer trigger. It is live, wired to the inbound line, and the knowledge base is kept current.

What it shows: the guardrails live in the knowledge base and its structure, where staff can read and edit them.

03

Agent as router

A clinic reference build, on demo data, used as the pattern for health and wellness clients.

Voice agent · workflows as effectors

The model decides which branch fires; workflows do the work

After a call the agent chooses one of five post-call handlers by natural-language trigger: confirmation and intake, no-show recovery, review request, cross-sell, or human escalation. Each handler is an ordinary workflow, so the clinic's staff can read and edit what happens next without touching the prompt. The build ships with a structured service catalogue (codes, durations, price bands, per-service prep text interpolated into the confirmation message) and a safety rail that routes emergencies to a human immediately. The clinic data in this account is fictional; the pattern is what is reused.

What it shows: keep the model's decision small and legible, and keep the consequences in a system a non-engineer can audit.

04

The orchestration state machine

The template every new client account is provisioned from.

Conversation AI · numbered, ordered units

AI.00 to AI.14

A numbered, dependency-ordered series of workflow units (AI.00 to AI.14) that covers the four things naive bot builds leave out: a debounce for double messages, a fallback when the model has nothing useful to say, a human kill-switch where one tag stops the bot and a person takes the thread, and externalized prompt loading, so the persona lives in configuration. The rest handle channel selection, conversation summarization into the contact record, and database reactivation. The same unit set, and the same handoff tags, appear in every account provisioned from the template.

What it shows: platform thinking inside a no-code tool.

05

Two external-system bridges

Integration under constraint: the CRM cannot traverse a relation, and the vendor's schema is not ours.

Integration · schema design

A recreation platform and a clinic EMR, flattened onto the contact record

For the members-based client, ten calendars and a field cluster are created programmatically from the practice-management system, with the raw record retained as JSON. For a med-spa on a Canadian clinic EMR, bidirectional sync workflows land a flattened appointment schema of about 95 fields on the contact, including the next two appointments side by side and a last-updated watermark. Three decisions, each with a reason. Denormalized: the CRM's workflows cannot walk a relation. Raw JSON kept: the vendor's schema changes without notice. Watermark kept, so a stale sync is visible.

What it shows: the reasons behind an ugly schema, stated.

06

Closed-loop attribution

The growth-operator half of the profile, evidenced in a schema.

Measurement · ad attribution on the contact

The click-to-revenue field set, next to lifetime revenue

On a med-spa account the contact record carries the click identifiers, the campaign, ad-group and ad ids, the full UTM set including match type, and the lifetime purchase total on the same row, driving a workflow that updates ROI per contact. A second account tracks offline sources (billboard, street sign, referral) alongside the digital ones in one twelve-option field, so paid and unpaid acquisition sit in a single report.

What it shows: attribution designed into the data model, so the numbers live where the actions do.

07

The LLM lineage, 2022 to 2026

Being early, and being right about the hard part.

History · what survived

From GPT-3 workflows to a voice agent, one pattern intact

A 2022 training account carries the first status-prediction fields. In May 2023 the studio's own account gained eight GPT-3 conversation workflows separated by channel and source, a parked wait-state, a per-contact token counter, and a workflow named for what mattered most: when the model hands off or falls back, tag the contact and call a webhook so a person takes over. The 2025 snapshot ships the same stop-bot and handoff tags. The 2026 voice agents route escalations the same way. Three years, two model generations, a full platform change, and the human-handoff pattern is the part that never changed.

What it shows: the escalation design was the durable idea; the models were replaceable.
